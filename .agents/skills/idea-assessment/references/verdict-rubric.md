# Gates, scores, and verdicts

## The six gates

Binary. Each one is a way solo-founder ideas reliably die, so a failure caps the verdict no matter how strong the rest of the analysis looks. Judge on evidence found, not on optimism about what could be true.

**1. Real, recurring, expensive pain.**
Something breaks or costs real money without this. Fails if the honest description is "nicer" or "more convenient" or if the pain occurs a few times a year.

**2. Identifiable buyer who can pay and can be reached by one person.**
You can name the role, the company type, or the person. Fails if the buyer is "consumers" generally, if the pain holder cannot authorise spend, or if reaching them requires an enterprise sales motion.

**3. Shippable v1 in 8 weeks or less, under roughly $5k out of pocket.**
One builder, no team, no raise. Fails on marketplaces needing both sides, unavailable data, regulated categories requiring compliance before launch, or hardware.

**4. Unit economics survive at a price the buyer will actually pay.**
Especially inference, API, and support cost per user against a realistic price point. Fails if gross margin at 100 users is negative or if the price required exceeds what the research shows this buyer pays for adjacent tools.

**5. A distribution channel reachable without a budget.**
A named community, an existing audience, a partner, a directory, a cold-outreach list, or a place the buyer already congregates. Fails if the plan is "SEO", "content", "go viral", or "word of mouth".

**6. Not trivially absorbed by an incumbent.**
Fails if a platform the buyer already pays for could ship this in a sprint and has reason to give it away free.

### Gate to verdict ceiling

| Gates failed | Highest possible verdict |
|---|---|
| 0 | BUILD NOW |
| 1 | TEST FIRST or RESHAPE |
| 2 | RESHAPE |
| 3+ | PASS |

Failing gate 1 (no real pain) means PASS regardless of the count. Nothing downstream fixes the absence of a problem.

## Scoring

Score 1-5 for texture, not as the decision mechanism. Gates decide; scores explain. Present as a compact table with a one-line justification per row.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain intensity | Mild annoyance | Real friction, tolerated | Something is actively broken and costing money |
| Buyer clarity | "Everyone" | A segment you can describe | A named list you could email this week |
| Willingness to pay | No evidence | Adjacent tools get paid for | This buyer already pays someone to solve exactly this |
| Competitive space | Crowded and funded | A few players, all mediocre | Dead or absent, for a reason you understand |
| Time to v1 | Quarters or more | 4-8 weeks | Days |
| Distribution edge | No access to the audience | A plausible channel | Existing audience or unfair access |
| Founder fit | No relevant domain or network | Adjacent experience | Lived the problem, knows the buyers |

Do not average into a single headline number. Averaging hides the one dimension that is a 1 and kills the business.

## The four verdicts

**BUILD NOW.** All gates pass, the thin v1 is weeks not quarters, and there is a named path to the first 20 users. Say what to build first and what to deliberately not build.

**TEST FIRST.** The idea is plausible but hinges on one unproven assumption, usually willingness to pay or reachability of the buyer. Name the specific test, the cost, the timebox, and the number that constitutes a pass.

**RESHAPE.** The broad idea fails but a narrower version clears the gates - a specific vertical, a smaller segment, a single workflow, a services-first wedge, or a different buyer with the same problem. Describe that version concretely enough to act on. This is often the most valuable verdict.

**PASS.** Structural problem that effort does not solve. Always name what would have to change for this to become a BUILD, so the user knows whether it is "no" or "not yet".

## Tone

Blunt, specific, no hedging language. "This is a feature, not a company" is a legitimate and useful sentence. So is "you are two years early".

Skepticism applies to the idea, not the founder. Never editorialise about the person's judgement for having the idea. The point of the exercise is to find the version worth building, and a fast honest no is worth more than a slow polite maybe.
