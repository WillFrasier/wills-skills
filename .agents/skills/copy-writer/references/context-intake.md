# Context Intake

Copy is only as good as the brief behind it. Work through this checklist before writing.
For each item, either **determine it** from the context you were given (screenshot, URL,
file, adjacent copy, the user's description) or **ask** about it. Items marked
**[load-bearing]** will change the substance of the copy, if you can't determine one,
you must ask before writing.

## The checklist

### 1. What is this copy? **[load-bearing]**
Its type and form: headline, subhead, body paragraph, CTA/button label, nav item, form
label, helper text, placeholder, empty state, error message, success message, tooltip,
onboarding step, legal/privacy line, notification, email subject, etc.

Why it matters: a button label and a body paragraph obey different rules. A CTA is a
verb phrase; a body paragraph carries the reasoning. Getting the type wrong gets
everything wrong.

### 2. Where does it live, and what's around it? **[load-bearing]**
The page/screen, the component, its position in the layout, and the copy immediately
adjacent to it (the heading above, the button below, the field it labels). If you have a
URL or file path, read it. If you have a screenshot, study the hierarchy.

Why it matters: copy is read in sequence. A subhead that repeats the headline, or a CTA
that contradicts the body, is a context failure, not a wording failure.

### 3. What is its role / function? **[load-bearing]**
What must this copy make the user **do**, **feel**, or **understand**? What happens if
it's absent or wrong? Is it the primary driver of an action, or supporting reassurance?

Why it matters: this is the single most important input. "Make them trust us" and "make
them click" produce different sentences.

### 4. Who is the audience? **[load-bearing]**
Who reads this? What do they already know? What do they care about? What's their
emotional state at this moment (curious, anxious, rushed, skeptical, excited)? Are they
new or returning? Technical or not?

Why it matters: the same product speaks differently to a first-time visitor and a power
user, to a worried parent and a busy admin.

### 5. What is the goal / success metric? **[load-bearing]**
The outcome the page optimizes for: sign-up, completion, activation, trust, clarity,
retention, support deflection. If the user gave a metric or business goal, use it.

Why it matters: it tells you what to prioritize when clarity and persuasion conflict.

### 6. What is the voice / vibe? **[load-bearing]**
The brand's personality and the emotional register this specific moment calls for. Warm
and human? Crisp and professional? Playful? Reassuring? Authoritative? Is there an
existing voice guide, or existing copy on the site to match?

Why it matters: "professional" means different things in a fintech app and a family app.
If you don't know the vibe, you'll default to generic, which is the failure mode.

### 7. What are the constraints?
Length or character limits, layout space, localization/i18n needs, legal or compliance
requirements, accessibility (screen-reader clarity, plain-language reading level),
SEO/keyword needs, existing design tokens or components, terminology that must stay
consistent with the rest of the product.

Why it matters: a beautiful line that doesn't fit the layout or breaks a legal
requirement is not usable copy.

### 8. What is the current copy, and why is it changing?
The existing text and the reason for the change ("isn't professional," "doesn't convert,"
"too long," "off-brand"). The complaint is a clue to the real problem.

Why it matters: users often describe a symptom. "Isn't professional" might mean "too
casual," "too corporate," "too vague," or "too long." Diagnose before you treat.

## How to ask well

- **Batch your questions.** Ask everything you need in one pass so the user answers once.
- **Lead with your best guess.** "I'd read this as a warm, low-pressure welcome for
  first-time users, correct me if the vibe should be more buttoned-up." Confirmation is
  faster than composition.
- **Ask only what's load-bearing.** Don't interrogate the user about trivia. State
  assumptions for low-stakes gaps and move on.
- **Offer a default.** If the user doesn't know the voice, propose one and proceed, so
  they're never blocked.
- **Show your work.** When you do infer, say what you inferred from ("based on the
  screenshot, this sits under the H1 and above the primary CTA, so I treated it as a
  supporting subhead").

## Minimal viable brief

If you must proceed with incomplete information, you need at least these four. Without
them, ask:

1. What the copy is (type).
2. Its role (what it must accomplish).
3. The audience.
4. The vibe.

Everything else can be assumed and flagged.
