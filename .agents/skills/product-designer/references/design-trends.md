# Design trends

Last reviewed: 2026-10-04. Refresh every 6 months. If older, tell the user the trend notes may be stale and offer to run a web search to update this file.

How to use: trends are context for judging fit, never a checklist to impose. Recommend one only when it serves the brief. A dated-looking treatment is not a flaw if it suits the audience.

## Durable principles (change slowly)
- Intent over decoration: every effect should earn its place by clarifying hierarchy, state, or action.
- Clarity has aesthetic value: legible structure, honest layout, visible grid.
- Users get control: motion, personalization, and AI should be optional and stoppable.
- Accessibility is a baseline, not a style: size, contrast, and structure are required across every trend below.

## Current directions (2026)

### Physical depth, used with restraint
- Glass, spatial layers, soft 3D and material cues are back, but calmer and selective.
- Keep opaque reading zones so text contrast survives translucency.
- Smell test: if depth does not help hierarchy or interaction, it is decoration.

### Type-first and structural layouts
- Strong grid plus a distinctive typographic voice replacing layers of ornament.
- Visible grids (thick lines, accent color), editorial layouts, large type, variable fonts.
- Raw, schematic or blueprint-like "intentional incompleteness" for technical or data products. Credible only when clarity survives.

### Handmade, textured, nostalgic
- Hand-drawn marks, stickers, scrapbook feel, texture and warmth.
- Early-web and Y2K nostalgia, with cleaner layouts than the originals.
- Bold color, strong contrast, anti-design character. Anti-design means character, not chaos.

### Motion that guides
- Quiet, purposeful motion: micro-interactions as feedback, scroll-driven storytelling, animated data.
- Expect a way to pause or reduce motion. Check prefers-reduced-motion support (not judgeable from screenshots).

### AI as optional copilot
- AI features present but opt-in, respectful of context, not an all-knowing autopilot.
- Generative or adaptive UI that explains what it did and lets users undo.

### Other signals
- Calm interfaces: lower cognitive load, fewer visual effects, visible defaults and clear content hierarchy (Envato).
- Minimalism with personality: bright accents, bold type, custom graphics, mascots.
- Buttonless or gesture-led UI on mobile (verify discoverability).
- Dark mode as a first-class theme, not an afterthought.
- Deeper personalization beyond "Hello, [Name]".
- Eco-conscious design: light assets, fewer heavy scripts.
- Returning skeuomorphism in specific material languages (wood, metal, manuscript). Niche; needs a strong reason.

## Dated or risky unless deliberate
- Gradient-everything hero sections with generic abstract blobs.
- Glass on every surface, low-contrast text over blur.
- Heavy parallax and autoplay motion with no stop control.
- Generic stock-illustration "Corporate Memphis" people.
- Placeholder-looking AI imagery with obvious tells.
